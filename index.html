About

TacEdge brings together researchers and practitioners working on sensing, communications and autonomy for contested environments: RF and optical surveillance, tactical telecommunications, and applied AI in the field.

Equipment in the field has to work with contested spectrum, intermittent links and whatever processing fits on the platform. The workshop is about methods that hold up under those constraints.

The morning is two keynotes and two sessions of short talks, free and open to everyone. The afternoon is three hands-on tutorials that every participant rotates through — paid, and limited to 40 places.

It is aimed at people who are not already specialists in these fields — Defence and defence-industry practitioners, researchers and students — who want a working grasp of the state of the art and of where it still falls short.

TacEdge 2026 is the first of what is intended to become an annual workshop.

Travelling for the space conference? TacEdge runs the day after the Australian Space Domain Awareness and Space Traffic Management Conference (10–12 November), at the same venue. Stay one more day.

Tutorials

Three in parallel · everyone does all three

Three tutorials run in parallel across three sessions, and every participant rotates through all three — nobody picks one and misses the others. No prior experience with radio, radar or machine learning is assumed. Bring a laptop.

RFT-01

Analysing the EM spectrum with SDR

The basics of software-defined radio and EM monitoring: capturing a live band, reading a waterfall, and working out what is transmitting, when, and how wide.

ImageryT-02

SAR and optical satellite imagery

Open-source intelligence from satellite imagery: taking Sentinel-1 radar data through calibration, speckle reduction and terrain correction, then reading it against optical imagery of the same ground.

DetectionT-03

Target detection with deep-learning detectors

Training models to detect targets in real imagery and to report how confident they are — including where that confidence misleads, and what breaks on small targets and limited data.
