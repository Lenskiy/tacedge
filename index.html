About

TacEdge brings together researchers and practitioners working on sensing, communications and autonomy for contested environments: RF and optical surveillance, tactical telecommunications, and applied AI in the field.

Equipment in the field has to work with contested spectrum, intermittent links and whatever processing fits on the platform. The workshop is about methods that hold up under those constraints.

The morning is two keynotes and two talk sessions. The afternoon is three hands-on tutorials across two parallel slots, and the day closes with a 105-minute autonomous robot capture-the-flag competition.

It is aimed at researchers, Defence and defence-industry practitioners, and graduate students. Demonstrations run continuously through the day, including passive RF sensing and an educational robot arena.

TacEdge 2026 is the first of what is intended to become an annual workshop.

Travelling for the space conference? TacEdge runs the day after the Australian Space Domain Awareness and Space Traffic Management Conference (10–12 November), at the same venue. Stay one more day.

03

Tutorials

3 sessions · 2 parallel slots

Three hands-on tutorials run in two parallel slots. Bring a laptop.

RFT-01

Analysing the EM spectrum with SDR

Working with software-defined radio to capture, visualise and characterise signals — from finding a carrier in the noise floor to identifying what is transmitting and when.

ImageryT-02

SAR and optical satellite imagery

Taking Sentinel-1 radar data through calibration, speckle reduction and terrain correction, then reading the result against optical imagery of the same ground — what each sensor shows, and what can actually be concluded from it.

DetectionT-03

Target detection with YOLO and beyond

Training and running modern detectors on real imagery, with attention to the constraints that matter at the edge — small targets, limited data, and inference on modest hardware.

04

Capture the flag

105 min · 2 v 2 · teams of 2–4

An asymmetric two-against-two autonomous robot game, run as the closing session of the day. Teams of two to four write the strategy; the robots execute it without human control.

One side attacks and the other defends, and the two sides do not have the same capabilities. Teams develop and test their attack and defence strategies during the session; nothing on the arena is driven by hand.

The build phase runs against a simulator rather than the arena, so every team can keep testing continuously. LLM assistance is available throughout it.

Qualifying runs go against a reference defender across parallel simulators. The top four teams go through to a knockout bracket on the physical arena, and the day closes with the awards.

Bring a laptop. The rules, the API documentation and the context pack for LLM assistance will be published here ahead of the event.
