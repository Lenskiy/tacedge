About

The tactical edge is where the assumptions break

Spectrum is contested, bandwidth is scarce, links drop, and the hardware that has to carry the load is whatever fits on the platform. TacEdge is about the methods and tools that still work under those conditions.

The morning is keynotes and talks. The afternoon is hands on — parallel tutorials in software-defined radio, satellite imagery, and object detection, run as working sessions rather than lectures. The day closes with an autonomous robot capture-the-flag competition.

The workshop is aimed at researchers, Defence and defence-industry practitioners, and graduate students. Demonstrations run continuously through the day, including passive RF sensing systems and an educational robot arena.

TacEdge 2026 is a pilot event. If it lands well, it becomes annual.

Travelling for the space conference? TacEdge runs the day after the Australian Space Domain Awareness and Space Traffic Management Conference (10–12 November), at the same venue. Stay one more day.

03

Tutorials

3 sessions · 2 parallel slots

Three hands-on tutorials run in two parallel slots. Bring a laptop.

RFT-01

Analysing the EM spectrum with SDR

Working with software-defined radio to capture, visualise and characterise signals — from finding a carrier in the noise floor to identifying what is transmitting and when.

ImageryT-02

SAR and optical satellite imagery

Reading synthetic aperture radar alongside optical imagery: what each sensor gives you, where speckle and geometry mislead, and how to get from raw product to something usable.

DetectionT-03

Target detection with YOLO and beyond

Training and running modern detectors on real imagery, with attention to the constraints that matter at the edge — small targets, limited data, and inference on modest hardware.

04

Capture the flag

105 min · 2 v 2 · teams of 2–4

An asymmetric two-against-two autonomous robot game, run as the closing session of the day. Teams write the strategy; the robots run it with no one at the controls.

One side attacks and the other defends, and the two sides do not have the same capabilities. Everything on the arena is autonomous: teams develop and test their attack and defence strategies during the session, then hand them over and watch them run.

The build phase runs against a simulator rather than the arena, so every team can keep testing continuously. LLM assistance is available throughout it.

Qualifying runs go against a reference defender across parallel simulators. The top four teams go through to a knockout bracket on the physical arena, and the day closes with the awards.

Bring a laptop. The rules, the API documentation and the context pack for LLM assistance will be published here ahead of the event — there will be nothing to read for the first time on the day.
