About

TacEdge is a one-day workshop on sensing, communications and autonomy in contested environments, pairing a morning of invited talks with an afternoon of hands-on tutorials. The subjects are RF and optical surveillance, tactical telecommunications, and applied AI in the field.

Equipment in the field has to work with contested spectrum, intermittent links and whatever processing fits on the platform. The workshop covers methods that hold up under those constraints.

The morning is two keynotes and two sessions of short talks, free and open to everyone. The afternoon is three hands-on tutorials that every participant rotates through, paid and limited to 40 places.

It is aimed at people who are not already specialists in these fields: Defence and defence-industry practitioners, researchers and students who want a working understanding of the state of the art and its open problems.

TacEdge 2026 is the first of what is intended to become an annual workshop.

Attending the space conference? TacEdge runs the day after the Australian Space Domain Awareness and Space Traffic Management Conference (10–12 November), at the same venue.

03

Tutorials

Three sessions · three tutorials

Three tutorials run in parallel across three sessions, and every participant rotates through all three. No prior experience with radio, radar or machine learning is assumed. Bring a laptop.

RFT-01

Analysing the EM spectrum with SDR

The basics of software-defined radio and EM monitoring: capturing a live band, reading a waterfall, and working out what is transmitting, when, and over what bandwidth.

ImageryT-02

SAR and optical satellite imagery

Open-source intelligence from satellite imagery: taking Sentinel-1 radar data through calibration, speckle reduction and terrain correction, then reading it against optical imagery of the same ground.

DetectionT-03

Target detection with deep-learning detectors

Working with YOLO and comparable detectors on real imagery: training them, reading the confidence they report, and seeing where that confidence misleads. The session then combines several detectors in an ensemble, which reduces misclassification and the amount an operator has to check by hand.

Competition

16:00 · to be confirmed
Robotics16:00

Robot capture-the-flag competition

The day closes with a knockout tournament on Plato Pod, a table-top arena built at UNSW Canberra in which small robots share one playing field with virtual ones. Eight teams of two take part. The early rounds are played in simulation; the final and the third-place match are played with real robots.
